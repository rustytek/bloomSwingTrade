10yr & 2yr Treasury Yields gold = 10yr · blue = 2yr
Plain English: These lines show what the market thinks money should cost over short and long time periods. The 2yr line reacts quickly to Fed policy expectations; the 10yr line reflects longer-term growth and inflation expectations.
What to watch: Rising 10yr yield signals growth/inflation expectations — good for banks, bad for long-duration growth stocks. When 2yr > 10yr the curve is inverted — a historically reliable recession signal within 12–18 months. 10yr > 2yr = normal, healthy lending environment. Watch for the spread crossing zero as a regime change indicator.
Yield Spread: 10yr − 2yr green = normal · red = inverted
Plain English: This subtracts the 2yr yield from the 10yr yield. Above zero usually means normal credit conditions; below zero means the curve is inverted.
What to watch: Positive spread = banks profit lending long-term → credit availability is healthy. Negative / inverted = banks have less incentive to lend, credit tightens → historically precedes recessions. The re-steepening after inversion (spread rising back toward 0) is often the period when recession actually hits — not the inversion itself.
M2 Money Supply billions USD
Plain English: M2 is a broad measure of cash and cash-like money in the economy. More liquidity can support risk assets; less liquidity can make rallies harder to sustain.
What to watch: Rising M2 = more liquidity in the financial system — bullish for equities, especially growth stocks and crypto. Falling M2 = liquidity draining — bearish for risk assets, supports cash/bonds. M2 rate-of-change (acceleration) matters more than level. A slowdown in growth can precede pullbacks even while M2 is still rising.
Federal Funds Rate %
Plain English: This is the Fed's main policy rate. Higher rates make borrowing more expensive; lower rates usually add stimulus and liquidity.
What to watch: Rising rates = tighter policy — raises the discount rate for stocks, pressures valuations, especially growth/tech. Cutting cycle = monetary stimulus — historically bullish for equities and real estate. The peak of a rate cycle is key: equities often rally before the first cut as markets price in easing. The rate level vs. inflation (real rate) matters most.

VIX — CBOE Volatility Index 20 = fear threshold · 30 = extreme fear
Plain English: VIX is the market's fear gauge. A low VIX means traders are calm; a high VIX means traders are paying up for protection.
Reading the VIX: VIX < 15 Complacency — market may be overextended, risk/reward deteriorating. VIX 15–20 Neutral — normal market environment. VIX 20–30 Elevated fear — contrarian buy zone, watch for reversals. VIX > 30 Extreme fear / panic — often marks short-term bottoms.
Key patterns: Rising VIX + falling price = sellers in control. Falling VIX + rising price = healthy uptrend. VIX spike + quick reversal = panic washout (bullish). VIX rising slowly while price rises = distribution warning.
